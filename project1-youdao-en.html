Project 01 · Government Affairs · 2026–Present

NetEase Youdao AI Policy Research & National Program Applications

My Role

Government Affairs Intern

Timeline

Sep 2026 – Present

Project Type

Technology Policy / Compliance / Program Applications

Focus Areas

AI Governance / Technology Compliance

01 · Context

In 2026, China's AI industry entered a phase of more standardized regulation. Policies from MIIT, the Cyberspace Administration of China and other authorities on AI service providers and generative AI compliance directly affected the design, launch and operation of products such as Youdao Dictionary Pens, AI Earphones and smart learning hardware. Meanwhile, the Internet Society of China launched a call for exemplary “AI+” cases, offering selected projects national-level recognition and resource support.

02 · Challenges

  • • Rapid, technically complex AI policy changes make it difficult for business teams to translate regulatory requirements into timely product adjustments, creating compliance risks.
  • • National “AI+” applications require technical, IP, product, market and implementation evidence from multiple departments under tight deadlines and demanding requirements, without centralized coordination.
  • • User complaints and feedback from the 12345 hotline and regulatory platforms in Guangzhou and Hangzhou are dispersed across business units, lacking unified records and follow-up mechanisms and increasing regulatory exposure.

03 · My Role

  • • Independently track national, Guangdong and Zhejiang AI policy documents, produce impact assessment reports, and recommend product compliance adjustments.
  • • Serve as a principal writer of “AI+” application materials for Youdao AI Earphones and the Digital Industrial Base, coordinating supporting evidence across departments.
  • • Establish regulatory complaint records for both cities, track cases daily, coordinate remediation with business teams, and prepare weekly compliance risk reports.

04 · Process

1. Building a Policy Monitoring System: Create daily policy monitoring records focused on AI documents from MIIT, the CAC, the NDRC and other authorities. Develop a standardized assessment template covering key requirements, affected products, compliance deadlines and recommended adjustments, and circulate weekly updates to product, engineering and legal teams.

2. Coordinating Program Application Materials: Review the “AI+” application guidelines and identify 12 assessment dimensions, including technical innovation, implementation outcomes, social value and intellectual property. Coordinate patents and algorithm documentation from engineering, feature descriptions from product, implementation data from marketing and qualifications from legal, consolidating evidence and drafting the applications.

3. Closed-Loop Compliance Risk Management: Connect complaint channels involving customer service, market regulation and cyberspace authorities in Guangzhou and Hangzhou. Maintain a unified tracker covering receipt, assignment, remediation, regulatory feedback and archiving, and share risk alerts on recurring issues with business teams for preventive improvements.

05 · Reflections

This experience challenged my earlier perception that government affairs was simply about relationships. I came to understand its value in technology companies: neither mechanically imposing policies at the expense of business nor pursuing growth without regard for rules, but translating between regulators and business teams. This means turning policy language into actionable requirements and presenting corporate innovation in ways aligned with policy priorities.

My greatest growth was learning to solve problems under multiple constraints. Regulatory boundaries, business needs and limited time require a careful balance—protecting compliance without unnecessarily restricting innovation.
